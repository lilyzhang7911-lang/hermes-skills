# Dialectical Design

Use when the user requires the design of complex, high-level systems, architectures, or conceptual frameworks that require both structural rigor and an appreciation for emergent complexity/chaos.

## Workflow
1. **Thesis (The Mechanistic/Ordered Approach)**: Propose a structured, highly organized, and logical solution. Focus on efficiency, clarity, and hierarchical order.
2. **Antithesis (The Dialectical Critique)**: Actively find the "structural death" in the Thesis. Identify biases (e.g., over-rationalization, mechanistic bias), identify where the structure kills the "vitality" or "chaos" of the subject, and identify the "cognitive friction" or "entropy" introduced by the design.
3. **Synthesis (The Evolutionary/Ecological Approach)**: Propose a final version that integrates the order of the Thesis with the vitality of the Antithesis. Aim for "Ordered Chaos" or "Dynamic Emergence" rather than static hierarchy.

## Pitfalls
- **Avoid "Mechanistic Bias"**: Do not just build a better hierarchy. If the user is philosophically driven, a better hierarchy is often just a prettier cage.
- **Avoid "Chaos for Chaos's sake"**: A design that is purely chaotic is useless. The goal *is* controlled emergence.
- **Don't skip the Antithesis**: A design without a critique is just a proposal. A dialectical design requires the tension of the counter-argument.

## Implementation Notes
- This skill is specifically designed for users who demand "high-entropy" reasoning and dialectical rigor.
- Always present the Thesis, Antithesis, and Synthesis as distinct, visible stages in the dialogue to demonstrate the cognitive work performed.
